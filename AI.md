# AI-assisted contributions

We're glad you're here. Kibana has always been built by people who care about
the product, and we want that to stay true as more of us reach for AI tools in
our daily work. This document explains how to contribute well when AI is part of
your process. None of it is meant to discourage you. We want your contributions,
and we want them to land.

## Talk to us before you build

The single most useful thing you can do is engage with the team early. Before
you write code, open an issue describing what you want to change, or leave a
comment on an existing one to say you're picking it up. This gives maintainers a
chance to point you at the right approach, flag work already in progress, and
save you from building something that can't be merged. A large pull request that
arrives with no prior conversation is hard to review and often has to be turned
away, no matter how much effort went into it. A short note up front avoids that.

## Keep changes focused

The contributions that move fastest are the ones with a clear, contained goal:
fixing a specific bug, improving a confusing piece of documentation, filling a
gap in test coverage, making a component more accessible. Pick something you can
hold in your head and validate from end to end yourself. Sprawling changes that
touch many areas at once are difficult to reason about and difficult to trust,
so when in doubt, do less.

## Understand what you submit

You should be able to explain every line in your pull request. Read the diff,
run the code, and confirm it actually does what you think it does. If a change is
there and you can't say why, it doesn't belong in the PR. This is the bar for all
contributions, and it doesn't change when a tool wrote the first draft. Reviewers
will ask questions, and "the AI generated it" is not an answer any of us can work
with.

## Let AI amplify you, not stand in for you

Used well, AI is a genuine accelerator. Lean on it to move faster through work
you already understand, to explore an unfamiliar corner of the codebase, or to
draft something you'll then shape and verify. What doesn't work is using it to
produce output you can't evaluate, or to generate conversations with the team
that you didn't actually write or think through. When you comment on an issue or
respond in review, we want your understanding, in your words. The tool is there
to sharpen your judgment, not replace it.

## Quality over quantity

We would much rather review one thoughtful, well-tested change than a pile of
auto-generated ones. Low-effort submissions that haven't been understood or
verified cost maintainers real time, and we will close them. That's not a
judgment of you as a contributor. It's how we keep the project healthy for
everyone. Bring us your best work on something you care about, and we'll meet you
there.
